# Research, October 2026: pipelines, models, retrieval

What other systems do, which models could replace Gemma 4 12B, and what Orb should test next. Compiled 2026-10-04
from three research passes (pipelines; small and quantized models; retrieval and serving) against what rounds 1-4
measured. Every claim below links to its source in the last section; items the research could not confirm are marked
*unverified*. Numbers from other papers use other models and corpora, so they show direction, not targets.

## Summary

- **Orb throws a note away when its extraction fails, and never shows the answering model the note itself.** A note
  node stores only its id and title; search covers extracted entities, relationships and context sentences. Every
  strong system in the literature keeps the raw passage searchable next to the graph: HippoRAG 2 lost 6-11 recall
  points without passage nodes, and a 12-method comparison calls retaining chunks "crucial". On MuSiQue, where half the
  gold is lost at extraction, this is the largest untested lever we have.
- **Spread from matched facts, not just from neighbours.** HippoRAG 2 links the whole query to embedded triple sentences
  (74.7 against 53.8 recall@5 on MuSiQue for matching entity names) and then runs personalised PageRank from those
  matches. Orb already does the first half: the question's vector searches the relationship and context sentences as
  well as the entities. What it does not do is spread through the graph from the matched facts; its expansion takes
  each hit's reranked neighbours.
- **Make the extractor's failures impossible, not rarer.** Constrained decoding removes code fences by construction
  (our largest MuSiQue failure: 59 of 78 rejects). A second pass that extracts relationships with the endpoint names
  constrained to an enum of the first pass's entities removes the other failure the same way. Neither repairs output.
- **Gemma 4 12B is the best extractor we have tested, and the best candidates to beat it are not smaller in memory but
  cheaper to run:** Gemma 4 26B-A4B (MoE, ~4B active, 14 GB QAT) and the Gemma 4 12B QAT build (same model, better 4-bit
  fidelity, 6.7 GB). Smaller and as good is plausible only for Phi-4 14B and IBM Granite 4.1 8B, both with strong
  structured-output records. Qwen 3.5 lost to Gemma at 50 questions; NuExtract3 (4B) is a specialist worth one run.
- **Two serving hazards we did not know about,** one already hit and fixed: llama-server's prompt cache can swap
  conversations between slots (#27148, fixed by `--cache-ram 0` before round 5 produced anything), and a grammar is
  silently not applied when a template's thinking is on (#20345). Our served runs keep thinking off and send the grammar
  directly, so they are on the safe side of both.

## 1. How pipelines like ours are built today

| System | Extraction | Entity merging | Retrieval | Reported (MuSiQue / 2Wiki / HotpotQA F1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Microsoft GraphRAG | one call per chunk, then "gleaning" (asks whether entities were missed, extracts more) | exact name | Leiden communities, map-reduce over summaries; local neighbourhoods | 38.5 / 58.6 / 68.6 (in HippoRAG 2's table) |
| LightRAG | one call, entities then relations in one reply; prompt requires relation endpoints to be listed entities | exact name | "dual-level" keywords against entity and relation vectors | collapses in HippoRAG 2's setup (1.6 / 11.6 / 2.4) |
| HippoRAG 2 | NER, then triples conditioned on the entities (Llama 3.3 70B) | synonym edges at cosine ≥ 0.8 | passage + phrase nodes; query-to-triple linking; LLM filters triples; personalised PageRank | **48.6 / 71.0 / 75.5** (70B reader, 1,000 questions each) |
| KAG | multi-step, schema-free plus schema-constrained | concept graph alignment | question decomposed into logical-form plans | 48.7 / 76.2 / 76.2 |
| MiniRAG | built for 1.5-4B models | – | chunk + entity graph, topology does the work so the small model reasons less | 48-53% accuracy vs LightRAG 35-40% with small models (own datasets) |
| LinearRAG (ICLR 2026) | **no LLM**: spaCy NER, entity-sentence-passage graph | – | activation through entity-sentence links, then PageRank | beats HippoRAG 2 on 2Wiki (own metric) |
| RAGU (2026) | two stages: entities validated, then relations whose endpoints must match them (Pydantic) | DBSCAN + LLM summary | – | extractor size 3B-14B moves QA by ≤1.5 points once entities are consolidated |

What this means for Orb:

- **Orb's design is closest to HippoRAG and LightRAG** (one LLM extraction per note, free relation types, endpoint rule,
  vectors plus graph), with a research loop on top that most of these systems do not have. Its distinctive choices, the
  strict no-repair rule and the per-entity "isolated context" sentence, are not in the literature; nobody else counts a
  malformed reply as a lost note, which makes our extraction numbers stricter than theirs.
- **Passages are kept by everyone who scores well.** GraphRAG, LightRAG and RAPTOR, which lean on summaries and graph
  facts alone, lose 5-10 F1 on factoid QA against plain dense retrieval. A 2025 study found only ~66% of HotpotQA answer
  entities end up in an LLM-built graph; running vector RAG and graph RAG side by side and concatenating gained +6.4%.
- **Two-pass extraction is the common answer to endpoint mismatches** (HippoRAG 1, KGGen, RAGU). Our round 4
  `task_split` run did worse (82 of 239 notes), but it was not constrained: the second pass could still name an entity
  the first never listed. With the endpoints as a JSON-schema enum, a mismatch cannot be produced.
- **Long passages lose extraction density.** GraphRAG extracted about twice as many entity mentions from 600-token chunks
  as from 2,400-token ones; SynthKG measured single-step extraction losing ~60% of triplet density as documents grow
  from 100 to 1,200 words. Our 1,000-token chunk test did worse (135 of 239) because a failed chunk still fails the
  whole note; the density question and the failure-unit question are separate.
- **Multi-hop answering with small models improves most from planning and evidence distillation**, not from more steps:
  question decomposition before the first search (KAG, "Plan Before Search"), and AutoRefine's distilled-evidence step
  between searches (+9 EM on MuSiQue at 3B over Search-R1). Our MuSiQue result fits: recall 0.50 but exact match 10%.
- **Evaluation norms:** HippoRAG-style papers use 1,000 dev questions per dataset with a corpus of the questions'
  supporting and distractor passages, report EM, F1 and passage recall@5, and often an LLM judge. With 8B-class models,
  MuSiQue F1 sits around 25-46. HotpotQA is saturated for retrieval (dense retrieval alone gets 94.5% recall@5), which
  is why our retrieval levers do not separate there; MuSiQue and 2WikiMultiHop have 15-20 points of headroom.

## 2. Models that could match or beat Gemma 4 12B

Gemma 4 12B is the best extractor of everything rounds 1-4 tested. The candidates below are ordered by how likely they
are to beat it in our harness, which is stricter than any published benchmark.

| Model | Size at Q4 | Why it is a candidate | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gemma 4 12B QAT** (unsloth UD-Q4_K_XL) | 6.7 GB | the same model trained for 4-bit; unsloth's fixed QAT files are 99.96% byte-exact to Google's weights | none beyond a re-run; Google's own `qat-q4_0` conversion was broken |
| **Gemma 4 26B-A4B** (MoE, 3.8B active) QAT | 14.2 GB | stronger than the 12B on knowledge and reasoning (MMLU-Pro +5.4), decodes like a 4B model; anecdotally the most precise extractor | on a 24 GB Mac it is at the Metal memory limit (one report: 2 tok/s when it swapped); one paper saw 98% unparsable raw output until prompts changed |
| **Phi-4 14B** | ~9 GB | 0% JSON parse failures in two independent studies; matches Gemma 4 31B on extraction value accuracy; MIT, no thinking mode | 16K context *unverified* |
| **IBM Granite 4.1 8B** | ~5 GB | the smaller-than-Gemma candidate: IFEval 87, built for RAG, no thinking mode; Granite 4.0 had a 0.985 JSON pass rate | relation extraction untested anywhere we found |
| **Mistral Small 3.x 24B** | ~14 GB | best raw knowledge-graph F1 of six models in one 2026 study, 0% parse failures | memory, like the 26B |
| **NuExtract3** (4B, Qwen 3.5 based) | ~2.7 GB | a dedicated extractor; template-driven JSON; beats Qwen 3.5 9B on its makers' benchmark | needs its own prompt format; nothing ties relation endpoints to entities |
| Qwen 3.5/3.6 35B-A3B (MoE) | 17-20 GB | best open model in one structured-output study (value accuracy 0.83) | NVIDIA-only for us; Qwen 3.5 9B already trailed Gemma on endpoint exactness |

For answering, the strongest leads are Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B (Qwen was our best MuSiQue answerer; 80-100 tok/s on a 3090),
Gemma 4 26B-A4B (one model for both roles), and Gemma 4 E4B QAT as the low-memory default (it already matches the 12B on
HotpotQA). Deprioritised: gpt-oss-20b (33% unparsable output in one study, reasoning cannot be switched off),
LFM2.5-8B-A1B (reasoning-only), Ministral 3 (weak extraction accuracy), Llama (no new small models), DeepSeek distills
(thinking-only, 81-100% parse failures in one study).

What else the model research found:

- **Thinking does not help extraction**, and hurts exact-format constraints, most of all in small models. Keep it off for
  extraction; it may help multi-hop answering, worth one A/B on MuSiQue.
- **Quantization:** instruction following holds through 4-5 bits and breaks at 3 (IFEval 78.9 at F16, 73.9 at Q3_K_S). A
  bigger model at Q4 usually beats a smaller one at Q8, but not below 4 bits. KV-cache quantization can wreck instruction
  following (IFEval 69.5 → 16.8 in one study); Gemma's KV quantizes especially badly. Keep the KV cache at f16.
- **The 24 GB Mac's real budget is about 16 GB** (Metal caps GPU memory at ~65-70% of RAM on machines with 36 GB or less).
  Models of 17 GB and up are NVIDIA-only unless users change a system setting, which an app cannot ask of them.

## 3. Retrieval models, structured output and serving

- **Embedders:** Qwen3-Embedding is still the best Apache-licensed family; the large step is 0.6B → 4B (+6.6 MTEB English
  retrieval), 4B → 8B adds almost nothing. Newer rivals are licence-blocked for an app (Jina v5: CC BY-NC) or have
  llama.cpp problems (EmbeddingGemma output differs from the reference, *unverified* whether fixed). One hazard to check:
  Qwen3-Embedding needs an end-of-text token appended; older GGUFs did not set it, which "can significantly hurt
  accuracy".
- **Rerankers:** Qwen3-Reranker 4B is the sweet spot (MTEB-R 69.8; 8B 69.0; 0.6B 65.8). Quantizing to Q4_K_M costs
  nothing measurable. A GGUF converted without the classifier head returns scores near 1e-23: worth a sanity check.
  jina-reranker v3/v3.5 (listwise, stronger on BEIR) is non-commercial.
- **Fusion:** a tuned weighted combination of normalised scores beats reciprocal-rank fusion; with a reranker after it,
  fusion matters only for recall at the candidate cutoff. Query expansion and HyDE hurt strong retriever-plus-reranker
  stacks; skip them.
- **Contextual indexing** (prepending a short, document-aware context to what is embedded) cut retrieval failures by 35-67%
  in Anthropic's study. Orb's extraction already writes such context; it would cost no extra model call.
- **Constrained decoding:** a grammar makes fences and invalid JSON impossible and does not hurt extraction (the
  "constraints hurt reasoning" result did not survive a re-run with identical prompts). The schema is invisible to the
  model, so the prompt must still describe it. llama.cpp's JSON-schema grammars support `enum`, lengths and patterns,
  but no cross-field rule, so "endpoints must be listed entities" needs the two-pass enum. llguidance replaces GBNF
  sampling at ~50 µs per token instead of 1-2 ms.
- **Serving hazards:** the prompt-cache swap (#27148, now off in our servers); grammar not applied with thinking on
  (#20345); assistant prefill rejected with thinking on; Gemma's sliding-window attention means no prompt-prefix reuse
  unless `--swa-full`, which is what made the Mac's served evaluation slower; and outputs vary with how many slots are
  busy even at temperature 0, so served runs need paired statistics rather than exact replays (our call cache covers
  reruns).

## 4. What to test next

Ranked by expected effect on the two goals: better ingestion and retrieval, and a smaller model that holds Gemma 12B's
quality. Each respects the no-repair rule.

1. **Raw note text as a searchable passage, next to the graph.** Embed and keyword-index each note (or each passage of a
   long note), add it as a passage node linked to its entities, and let the research loop read the passage text with
   the entity facts. A failed extraction then costs graph edges, not the note. Measure gold-in-search on MuSiQue
   (currently 45-60%), recall and EM/F1. *A product change: the note text becomes part of retrieval and of what the
   model reads.*
2. **Two-pass extraction with endpoint enums.** Pass 1 returns entities; pass 2 returns relationships whose
   `source_name`/`target_name` are constrained to the pass-1 names by a JSON-schema enum, both passes grammar-constrained.
   Measure notes accepted (MuSiQue: 161/239 base, 173 with the reminder), relationships per note, gold indexed.
3. **Grammar-constrained extraction everywhere, with the schema in the prompt.** Round 4's constrained MuSiQue build was
   running ahead of the base (73% accepted against 67%); round 5 tests it with the reminder. Follow with a JSON-schema
   grammar (not just "any JSON") and llguidance on the rented GPUs.
4. **Graph spreading from matched facts.** Orb's vector search already matches the whole question against relationship
   and context sentences. Add personalised PageRank over Kuzu, seeded from the matched facts' entities (and passages,
   once item 1 exists), as HippoRAG 2 does, in place of or alongside per-hit neighbour expansion. Measure MuSiQue
   recall@5 and the multi-hop share of misses.
5. **Plan, then distil, in the research loop.** Step 0 decomposes the question into ordered sub-questions; each step
   carries a short distilled-evidence field forward; the final answer field is short, with reasoning before it. Measure
   MuSiQue EM/F1 by hop count, which separates answering failures from retrieval ones.
6. **Gemma 4 12B QAT and Gemma 4 26B-A4B QAT as extractors.** The QAT 12B is a free fidelity gain at a smaller file; the
   26B-A4B is the most likely model to beat the 12B, at the cost of memory, and it decodes faster. Run both on the
   served HotpotQA and MuSiQue sets.
7. **Phi-4 14B and Granite 4.1 8B as extractors** (and NuExtract3 4B if its template can carry our schema). These are the
   candidates for "as good as the 12B, smaller or cheaper".
8. **Qwen 3.6 35B-A3B as the answering model** (thinking off, then on), on MuSiQue, against Qwen 3.5 9B and E4B.
9. **Gleaning on long notes.** One follow-up call per long note asking for entities and relationships it missed, same
   constrained schema. Measure gold-answer entities in the graph against extraction time.
10. **Embedding hygiene and size.** Check our Qwen3-Embedding GGUFs append the end-of-text token; then 0.6B vs 4B vs 8B on
    MuSiQue first-stage recall@50, and Matryoshka truncation (1024 → 512 → 256) for index size on the desktop.
11. **Evaluate on 2WikiMultiHop and larger MuSiQue samples.** HotpotQA cannot separate retrieval configurations any more.
    Adopting the HippoRAG protocol (1,000 questions, supporting plus distractor passages) would also make our numbers
    comparable with the literature.
12. **Serving efficiency:** `--swa-full` for Gemma during evaluation (prompt reuse across research steps), llguidance,
    and n-gram speculative decoding for repetitive JSON. These change speed, not answers.

Items 1, 2, 4 and 5 change the product's pipeline, not just a setting, so they need a decision before they are built;
items 3, 6-8 and 10-12 are settings, models or harness work that can go into the next round directly.

## 5. Follow-up, 2026-10-05: AstaBrief, Laya, and other recent models

- **AstaBrief-8B (AllenAI, 2 Oct 2026)** is Qwen3-8B fine-tuned (SFT then DPO, targets written by frontier models) to
  write cited scientific reports from retrieved excerpts: 16K training context, Apache 2.0, community GGUFs only
  (mradermacher, including imatrix). Its own benchmark (ScholarQA-CS2) is strong for its size; nothing is published on
  instruction following, JSON, extraction or HotpotQA/MuSiQue. It is trained to write long markdown with `[ID]`
  citations in its own prompt format. **Extraction: no.** **Answering: a long shot**: Orb's loop wants a short JSON
  answer or next query, and the model was never trained to plan searches. Worth one cheap answering run at most.
- **Laya (ConvAI)** is not a language model in the generative sense: a 0.3-0.4B ModernBERT/mmBERT classifier that
  returns calibrated probabilities over a typed question (choice, score, yes/no) in one pass. It cannot write entity
  names, answers or queries. **Neither role.** At most it could serve as a cheap yes/no gate inside the loop, outside
  llama.cpp; not worth building now.
- **Granite 4.2 8B (IBM, 25 Aug 2026)** replaces Granite 4.1 8B on the shortlist: Apache 2.0, 128K context, thinking can
  be switched off, reinforcement learning aimed at instruction following and structured output, official GGUFs. The
  strongest "smaller than Gemma 12B" candidate for both roles.
- Also new: Qwen3.8-27B (dense, thinking on by default, ~16 GB at Q4: a ceiling reference, not a candidate); a 5.9 GB
  ternary compression of it (Ternary Bonsai 2 27B, llama.cpp support *unverified*); MiniCPM5-2B (likely too small for
  strict extraction). No new extraction specialist since NuExtract3.

## 6. Video: replacing Marlin 2B

- **What Marlin is:** NemoStation's fine-tune of Qwen3.5-2B (May 2026, Apache 2.0) for structured scene/event captions
  with timestamps and for finding a text query's span in a video; 2 fps, up to 240 frames (~2 minutes), ~448² per
  frame, transformers only. **It publishes no benchmark numbers**: its "tops CaReBench" claim does not appear on the
  official leaderboard, and its DREAM-1K placement implies roughly 36 F1 (below Tarsier2-7B at 42.0). The only firm
  proxy is its base, Qwen3.5-2B: Video-MME 69.0 without subtitles, MVBench 64.9 (thinking mode).
- **Smaller costs quality in this class; the bigger lever is the runtime.** Marlin runs only through transformers; the
  alternatives below run 4-bit through llama.cpp (video input landed June 2026, tested on Qwen3-VL-2B and Gemma 4 E4B)
  or MLX on Apple Silicon. The cheapest speed-up needs no model change: Marlin's cost grows with frames, so halving the
  frame rate or pixel budget roughly halves the work.

| Candidate | Params | Video-MME (no subs) | MVBench | Runtime | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3.5-0.8B | 0.8B | 57.7 | 55.8 | transformers now; GGUF + mmproj, MLX | Marlin's own family and pipeline, 2.5x smaller; a real quality drop |
| MiniCPM-V 4.6 | 1.3B | not published | not published | official GGUF, llama.cpp, Ollama | built on Qwen3.5-0.8B with 4-16x visual token compression, ~1.5x its speed; must be benchmarked |
| Qwen3-VL-2B Instruct | 2B | 61.9 | 61.7 | official GGUF, llama.cpp video, MLX | same size as Marlin, the safest 4-bit Metal path |
| SmolVLM2 500M / 2.2B | 0.5 / 2.2B | 42.2 / 52.1 | 39.7 / 46.3 | llama.cpp, MLX | fastest and smallest, clearly weaker |
| Qwen3.5-4B (an upgrade, not smaller) | 4B | 76.9 | 71.2 | as Qwen3.5-0.8B | beats every 2B model on published scores at ~3 GB in 4-bit |

- **These multiple-choice benchmarks do not measure Orb's task** ("explain what happens"). Captioning benchmarks do:
  DREAM-1K (event-level precision/recall by an LLM judge), CaReBench (spatial and temporal caption F1) and VDC (long
  reference captions checked through question-answer pairs). A fair comparison for Orb: 50-100 clips from DREAM-1K or
  CaReBench plus typical user videos, the same frame rate and pixel budget for every model, one local judge model, and
  seconds per minute of video and peak memory recorded. Marlin's captioning fine-tune could put it well ahead of the
  general models on that task; only such a run can say.
